## Interpretation

The autoBatch scenario measures the combination of `autoBatch`, `maxAutoPrepare`, and
`pipelineBatchWrites` against upstream pipelining. Its +57.4% median is the only result in
this full run that exceeds the comparator's same-code noise threshold with every round
moving in the same direction. It does not isolate the contribution of autoBatch alone.
No slowdown meets that threshold in this run. Unmarked positive medians are inconclusive.

The isolated pipeline-write scenario was repeated for 6 rounds of 3 seconds, with 1 second
of warmup per arm: [longer repeat](results-pipeline-writes-2026-11-13.md). The median changed
from -11.1% to +12.9%, with a 0.98–1.17x range and ±13.2% same-code noise in the longer run.
Neither a reliable gain nor a reliable regression is established for this option.
These measurements describe this machine and workload, not a universal speedup.

## Reproduction

Install the locked dependencies and build both checkouts, then point `PGHOST`, `PGPORT`,
`PGUSER`, and `PGDATABASE` at a dedicated benchmark database. The benchmark drops its own
`benchmark_rows` and `benchmark_scratch` tables in that database.

```sh
node benchmark/compare.js --base ../node-postgres-upstream --head . --rounds 4 --duration 1 --warmup 0.5 --output benchmark/results-local.md
```

For the longer measurement, use `--rounds 6 --duration 3 --warmup 1`; the saved repeat
selected only `select parameter, pipelineBatchWrites x10` while retaining the same workers,
A/B ordering, measurement loop, and noise calculation.
